# Supplied study notes

These are **author-created fictional teaching notes**, not quotations from a
textbook or peer-reviewed sources. Use them to practise grounded synthesis.
For a real assignment, locate and verify appropriate academic sources.

## S1: recognising versus retrieving

Reading an explanation can make it feel familiar. That feeling does not establish
that you can explain or apply it without looking. One way to check is to close
the explanation, answer a question, and compare your answer with the source.
In this exercise, use a small self-test rather than copying the explanation.

## S2: a realistic study block

A useful study block has a concrete task, an available amount of time, and a
checkpoint. "Study JavaScript" is hard to finish. "Write a filter predicate,
try two inputs, and explain the result" gives you something observable.
Equal time allocation is a simple planning aid, not a guarantee of learning.

## Questions to practise

1. What does familiarity with an explanation fail to demonstrate?
2. Rewrite "work on the assignment" as a task with a checkpoint.
3. What can this directory support, and what cannot it establish?
4. Which source note supports each claim in your draft?
